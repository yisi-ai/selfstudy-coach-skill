# Learn from supplied images

Use the user's images as source material for their current learning goal. Adapt to the notes and request; the principles below are not a required sequence or output template.

- **Read the actual images.** Use the host's image-understanding capability. Interpret relevant writing, formulas, diagrams, arrows, and corrections together. If an image is inaccessible, say so and ask for the relevant material; do not infer its contents from a filename or require manual transcription when the image is readable.
- **Organize only as much as needed.** Choose useful units and relationships from the material and the user's goal. Merge duplicates when helpful, preserve meaningful differences, and infer order only when supported. Do not assume one image is one topic or question, impose fixed categories or question counts, or transcribe every page before helping. A requested quiz or explanation needs no separate summary-approval step.
- **Keep uncertainty local.** Reinspect unclear details when possible. If a symbol, condition, or relationship could change an answer or explanation, ask a focused clarification or leave that item out while continuing with clear material. Do not turn a guess into a reference answer. State material coverage gaps without treating the available notes as a complete course.
- **Preserve meaning and distinguish additions.** Keep enough source cues in the task context to revisit a claim, such as an image number, heading, or nearby phrase; no fixed record format is required. Distinguish what the notes say from interpretation and supplementary teaching. Notes can contain mistakes: explain a supported correction or unresolved inconsistency rather than silently reproducing or rewriting it.
- **Choose the learning aid for the task.** Honor the requested form and depth. Use quizzes to check understanding and application, and whiteboards when intermediate steps or visual relationships help explain something. Neither is mandatory. Keep generated questions self-contained with supported answers, and focus each whiteboard on one coherent topic. Reuse the existing content formats and connection.
- **Send derived content to Web.** Source photos and their crops stay out of the Web workflow: do not upload, host, embed, or forward them as files, URLs, or Base64. Express the relevant knowledge using text, formulas, and supported diagram or animation objects. Do not rely on “see the uploaded photo” in a Web activity. If essential visual detail cannot be represented faithfully, explain that part in the conversation or clarify it instead of inventing a replacement.
