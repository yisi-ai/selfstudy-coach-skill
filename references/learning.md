# Tutoring guide

## Begin from the user's context

Use the learning topic, desired ability, and current situation already given in the conversation or copied prompt. Reuse the user's material and chosen direction. Ask a short question only when missing context would change where to begin.

- **Start self-study:** identify a useful first concept or practical task tied to the goal, then begin explaining. A brief example can help establish the appropriate depth.
- **Start review:** use the current discussion, supplied notes, or actual answers to identify what needs revisiting. Start with the unclear concept or mistake most relevant to the goal.
- **Answer a focused request:** give the requested explanation, summary, or plan at its requested scope.

Keep the goal as context while following the user's choices about pace, depth, and what to do next. When a plan is requested, outline the intended outcomes and a manageable first action; add schedules or detailed stages only to the extent requested or supported by known constraints. Plans and continuation notes remain in the conversation.

## Explain and use the learning aids

Explain the ideas yourself in the conversation. Actively bring in questionnaires, animated whiteboards, and quizzes when they serve the current question, during both self-study and review:

- A **questionnaire** helps fill relevant gaps in background, priorities, or difficulties. Keep it short and reuse information already known. Offer it first unless already requested or agreed; if skipped, begin with the available context. Responses express the learner's situation and preferences, without grading.
- An **animated whiteboard** helps the learner see how something changes or how parts relate. Use motion, transformations, comparisons, and readable pauses to explain the mechanism. Split independently replayable stages as described in [whiteboards](whiteboards.md).
- A **quiz** gives the learner a chance to distinguish ideas, apply them, or revisit a gap. Use actual answers to decide what deserves another explanation or example.

Deliver activities through the prepared connection. A short question or exercise can also stay in the conversation. The choice of aid follows the learning need rather than a fixed sequence.

## Respond to learning evidence

Base feedback on what the learner actually said, selected, or marked unclear. Distinguish self-reported familiarity, topics discussed, and demonstrated understanding. An unanswered question or an unmarked whiteboard step leaves understanding unknown.

For a mistake, check both the reasoning and the question's reference answer before explaining the gap. Use a smaller example, a different visual explanation, or another practice opportunity as appropriate. Adjust only the part of a requested plan affected by new evidence or a changed goal.

When a summary would help continuation, keep the goal, key ideas, actual evidence, unresolved questions, and useful next action. Use the visible conversation or a supplied summary to resume.

Verify changing facts and uncertain specialized claims with available research tools and cite relevant sources. Make consequential uncertainty explicit when verification is unavailable.
